Topic 01

Square Root

The square root of a number is a value that, when multiplied by itself, gives the original number.

Build the idea

Think of a square as a number multiplied by itself.

√
√25 = 5 because 5 × 5 = 25

The symbol √ is called the square-root symbol. When we write √25, the principal square root is the non-negative value 5.

√1 1 × 1 = 1 → 1
√4 2 × 2 = 4 → 2
√9 3 × 3 = 9 → 3
√16 4 × 4 = 16 → 4
√100 10 × 10 = 100 → 10
⚠️
Negative number reminder A negative number does not have a real square root. For example, √−9 is not a real number.

Topic 02

Cube Root

Cube roots work with three factors of the same number. Positive and negative values can both have real cube roots.

Build the idea

Think of a cube as a number multiplied three times.

∛
∛27 = 3 because 3 × 3 × 3 = 27

The symbol ∛ represents a cube root. To find ∛x, look for the number that produces x when used as a factor three times.

∛8 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 → 2
∛27 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 → 3
∛64 4 × 4 × 4 = 64 → 4
∛−8 (−2) × (−2) × (−2) = −8 → −2
∛−27 (−3) × (−3) × (−3) = −27 → −3
💡
Why can cube roots be negative? Three negative factors multiply to a negative result: (−2) × (−2) × (−2) = −8.

Topic 03

Positive & Negative Numbers

Use the number line to compare values and learn the main rules for working with signs.

Positive numbers

Values greater than zero.

+

Positive numbers include 1, 2, 3, 4, 10, 100, and so on. A plus sign may be written, but it is often omitted.

+7 = 7

On a number line, positive numbers are positioned to the right of zero.

Negative numbers

Values less than zero.

−

Negative numbers include −1, −2, −3, −10, and −100. The minus sign tells us the value is below zero.

−7 < 0

On a number line, negative numbers are positioned to the left of zero.

Addition & subtraction

Compare signs before combining values.

＋
−4 + (−3) = −7
  • 1 The numbers have the same sign.
  • 2 Add the absolute values: 4 + 3 = 7.
  • 3 Keep the negative sign → −7.
7 + (−3) = 4
  • 1 The signs are different.
  • 2 Subtract 3 from 7: 7 − 3 = 4.
  • 3 The larger absolute value is 7, so the result is positive.

Multiplication & division

Same signs make positive answers.

×÷
+ × + = +

Positive × positive → positive

+ × − = −

Positive × negative → negative

− × + = −

Negative × positive → negative

− × − = +

Negative × negative → positive

The same sign pattern applies to division. For example: −12 ÷ −3 = +4.
Topic 04

BODMAS

BODMAS helps you choose the correct order for solving a mathematical expression.

The order of operations

Work through the expression in a consistent order.

B
B Brackets ( )
O Orders Powers / roots
D Division ÷ /
M Multiplication × *
A/S Add / Subtract + −
Important: Division and multiplication share the same priority, so work from left to right. Addition and subtraction also share a priority, so work from left to right.

Worked example 01

Multiplication comes before addition.

01
3 + 4 × 2
  • 1 Multiply first: 4 × 2 = 8.
  • 2 Add: 3 + 8 = 11.
  • 3 Answer = 11

Worked example 02

Brackets and powers come first.

02
(6 + 2) × 3²
  • 1 Brackets: 6 + 2 = 8.
  • 2 Order: 3² = 9.
  • 3 Multiply: 8 × 9 = 72.
  • 4 Answer = 72

Simplify a square root

Not every number is a perfect square, but many can still be made simpler. This tool breaks a number into its prime factors and pairs them up to pull whole numbers out of the root.

Try a number

Prime factors

How it works

A square root asks which number multiplies by itself to give the value inside. So whenever a prime factor appears twice, one copy of it can step outside the root sign.

For example, 72 breaks down into 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3. There is a pair of 2s and a pair of 3s, so a 2 and a 3 come out, leaving one lonely 2 inside. That gives 6√2.

Green pills below the input are factors that found a partner.
